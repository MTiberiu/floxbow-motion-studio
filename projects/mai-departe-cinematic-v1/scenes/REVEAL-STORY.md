# Reveal story direction

## Active proof

The first 30 seconds use the prepared path-and-grid composition with new 2.5D scenery. A cold valley sits behind the three saved audio paths: atmospheric sky, a distant mountain range, a closer forested rock slope, a separate lake surface and a foreground shore. Sparse fish and birds animate independently. The prepared left-side reveal, soft edge and three route-aligned spray halos are retained and applied as one mask to all scenic layers. The 6.5-second trail keeps the markers separated from the reveal front.

## Direction for later sections

Use saved section and lyric anchors without changing their timing. Opening: incomplete rock and mist emerge from white. Burden/call: narrow deep currents and steep ridges, with a short tense weather accent if useful. Decision/refren: valley opens, water flows and vegetation begins. Rejection: fractured ground and cooler shadows, with earlier growth retained. Return: branching waterways, richer forest and small groups of birds. Final: broad, warmer horizon and calm water while the future grid remains open.

Three paths jointly build matter/relief, light/color and water/life. These are art-direction roles, not inferred emotional labels. The initial proof is limited to 30 seconds; after it, the existing vector treatment continues via the preserved legacy component. It does not claim the full-song narrative is finished.

The independent scenic layers are project-local assets in `assets/`; the water, atmosphere and wildlife are code-built. `JourneyWorld.tsx` uses `ThreeCanvas` with an orthographic camera, stable layer coordinates and small depth-based parallax. The generated cutouts are used separately; the full reference photograph is not used as a reveal plate or repeated texture.
